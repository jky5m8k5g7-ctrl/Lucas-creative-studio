# Hold That Thought

_Waiting for Lucas at the concept gate · project TEST_SOLACE_3_

## Lucas's idea

> Solace makes a $38 ceramic pour-over dripper, glazed by hand, one cup at a time. The ten minutes of making coffee by hand are the only part of a parent's morning nobody interrupts.

## Developed

**The only ten minutes of a parent's morning nobody interrupts: each day one child toes a tape line with news that can't wait, until the last drop leaves the Solace dripper and out it comes.**

Format: social_series. Social series, because the idea runs on a situation that repeats with a new variable: the same pour and the same tape line, with a different child holding different news each time. That is one child at a time, the way the brand's own words say 'one cup at a time'. Seeing it again is what proves it happens every morning, without a voiceover saying so, and each episode pays off in 30 s with the sound off. Runner-up: a single 45 s ad spot. It lost because one morning reads as a one-off story. Lucas's line is about a routine, and the rule only reads as a rule the second time we see it.

In a kitchen mid-breakfast, a parent of three works through the only ten minutes of the morning nobody interrupts: kettle, grind, a slow hand pour into a Solace dripper, one cup. Masking tape crosses the floor, laid over an older strip worn gray. Nobody explains it. Each episode opens as the kettle goes down and the coffee starts to drain: one child reaches the tape with news that can't wait and stops, toes on the line. First the youngest, fist clenched around something. Then the eldest, holding out a permission slip and an uncapped pen at arm's length. Then the middle child, a hand pressed flat to their forehead. The parent clocks each of them and watches the dripper. From the tape the child sees the dripper, never the cup; we see the last drops fall. On the last drop the parent lifts the dripper off the cup, and the news comes out: the fist opens on a tooth; the slip gets signed against the fridge, cup never set down. In the finale, the hand comes down from the forehead on bangs the child has cut themself, and the parent takes a long first sip before saying anything at all.

What makes it this idea and nobody else's:

- Kept: 'The ten minutes of making coffee by hand are the only part of a parent's morning nobody interrupts.' The logline opens on that line, and every episode takes place inside those ten minutes.
- Obvious version: a frazzled parent in a noisy kitchen finds calm over a hand pour while the kids' chaos blurs in the background, then smiles into the first sip.
- Differs by who, and by what it costs them: the calm is not the parent's escape but a truce the kids keep. They stand at the tape holding a lost tooth, an unsigned slip or home-cut bangs, and they pay for the parent's ten minutes by staying quiet.
- Product role: the dripper is the clock. The viewer's cue is its last drop and the child's cue is its lift, and a coffee machine would give nobody a reason to stand still. Swap in any other cone dripper and the clock still runs, so the brand's own words carry the difference. 'Glazed by hand, one cup at a time' is the rhythm of the series: one child, one piece of news, one cup, one morning. The end frame puts those exact words under the dripper, so each episode reads as the phrase acted out.
- Added detail: the tape is re-laid over an older strip worn gray, and nobody ever explains it. The rule reads as years old without a word (taste notes: restraint over explanation; one real detail only this family has).
- Taste, followed and broken on purpose: episodes 1 and 2 show the child as a fist, a pen and a slip (hands before faces). Episode 3 breaks that note because the bangs are the joke; A15 is the fallback. Scene sound only, no music. The product is a tool from the first second and a hero only on the end frame.
- Alternative (not briefed): one morning the kids make the pour for the parent. That is a new central situation, so it is not developed here.

Story:

- **protagonist**: A parent of three whose one uninterrupted stretch of the morning is a hand pour through a Solace dripper.
- **want**: To finish the pour and take the first sip before the day starts asking for things.
- **obstacle**: Each morning one child arrives at the tape line with news that can't wait.
- **turn**: On the last drop the parent lifts the dripper off the cup, and the news comes out.
- **ending**: In the finale, the hand comes down from the forehead on bangs the child has cut themself, and the parent takes a long first sip before saying anything at all.
