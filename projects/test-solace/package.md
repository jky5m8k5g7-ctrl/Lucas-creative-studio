## Developed

**For the ten minutes she makes coffee by hand, nobody interrupts a mother of three: her family queues behind a tape line until her Solace dripper's last drop, then crosses before her first sip.**

Format: ad_spot. Ad spot (A2): one proposition paid off by one turn in 60 s. The queue builds behind the tape and the clack releases it. Runner-up: social series, one family member per episode. School morning. Across the kitchen doorway runs a strip of blue painter's tape, scuffed grey in the middle where toes land, beside the pale ghost of an older line 40 cm nearer the counter. A mother of three fills the kettle; as its rumble rises, her daughter skids onto the tape in socks, permission slip and pen at arm's length. Nobody crosses. Nobody speaks. She sets her Solace dripper on the lopsided mug her eldest glazed, then grinds by hand; her son hops up in one shoe, its pair under the table on her side. The kettle clicks; the bloom hisses. Her partner joins the back, the toddler on his hip holding out an unpeeled banana like her big sister's slip. As she pours, the son's toe crosses the line and snaps back: his sister has hooked his hood, pen still in her fist. She never turns. The last drop falls; she lifts the dripper off and sets it down with a clack, and the queue crosses at once: without turning, she points under the table, and the slip, the shoe with its laces trailing and the banana reach her elbow while the full mug waits.
