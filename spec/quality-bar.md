# The studio's quality bar

Every department's work is scored 1–10 on four criteria before Lucas sees it. The grade is
**A** only when every criterion is 8 or higher and no code check fails. Work below A is
revised with the reviewer's notes, up to three rounds; whatever is still below A reaches Lucas
flagged, never hidden.

Reviewers quote the exact line that earns each score. A score without a quoted line doesn't
count.

## The anchors (same for every criterion)

| Score | Means |
|---|---|
| 10 | Best in class. You'd show it to other studios as the standard. Approve unchanged. |
| 9 | Approve unchanged. Nothing a senior practitioner would change. |
| **8** | **A. A senior practitioner would send it to the client today with only small notes.** |
| 7 | Competent, but a reader can see where the effort stopped. Real notes needed. |
| 6 | Generic: correct, but could belong to anyone's project. |
| 5 or below | Not usable. Missing parts, wrong brief, or wrong craft. |

Reviewers start below 8 and let the page argue them up. An 8 is earned, never the default.

## The four criteria

**Specificity: can someone act on it without asking a question?**
- 8+: names, numbers, objects, actions and sounds. "She wipes the pan with a paper towel
  folded in quarters; the towel comes away bronze." A crew could shoot it.
- 6: moods and adjectives. "A warm, inviting kitchen moment." Nobody knows what to build.

**Distinctiveness: could it only belong to this idea?**
- 8+: swap in a competitor's product or another brief and the work breaks. It has at least
  one detail nobody else would have thought of.
- 6: the swap works fine. It is category work: sunrise, smiles, slow-motion pour.

**Fit: does it deliver what was asked?**
- 8+: Lucas's idea is recognizable in it, sharper than he wrote it. It serves the brief, the
  route in production and the single-minded proposition. It uses only verified facts, includes
  every must-include, avoids every must-avoid.
- 6: a good piece of work for a different brief, or it quietly drops a requirement.

**Craft: would a senior practitioner in this role sign it?**
- 8+: correct technique for the role (timing that can be performed, lenses that exist,
  continuity that holds, rights that are cleared), nothing physically implausible, and every
  cross-reference to another department's IDs is right.
- 6: an amateur mistake a professional would catch on first read.

## What always costs points

- Banned language (the list in the workflow: elevate, seamless, journey, curated, stunning,
  cinematic, immersive, unlock, effortless, discover, "more than just", and the rest) or its
  cousins.
- A claim that isn't in the verified facts, stated as fact.
- A real person's name, face, voice or likeness as a reference for a fictional character.
- Stock images: the window-light mug, the slow-motion hair toss, the product spinning on a
  plinth, a laughing group at a table, a sunrise, a drone shot as a transition.
- Describing the feeling instead of the thing that causes it.
- Ending on a logo with no idea attached to it.

## What earns the top of the scale

- One real detail nobody asked for that makes the whole thing believable.
- Restraint: the piece leaves out the obvious beat and trusts the audience.
- Every department's choices visibly serve the same idea; you could guess the route from any
  single page.
- The product (or protagonist) earns its moment: it solves something we watched happen.

## The package panel

After the departments pass, three reviewers read the whole package blind to each other:

1. **Creative director**: would I put this in front of the client (or commissioner) today?
   Uses this bar and the studio's taste notes.
2. **Film director**: can I shoot it next week from these pages without a follow-up call?
3. **Client** (ads) or **commissioner** (narrative): does it sell this product, or is it a
   story an audience will stay for, and is it clearly ours?

The package is A when all three would approve it and every criterion averages 8 or higher.
Their notes go back to the departments responsible. Questions about the direction itself (the
idea's development, the strategy, the choice of route) go to Lucas, because that's his call.
