# Taste notes

> **EXAMPLE NOTES — written by Claude to test the taste mechanism, not Lucas's own.**

## What we like

- **Restraint over explanation.** If the picture already says it, the words stay quiet. The
  best spots trust the viewer to get it.
- **Hands before faces.** Show a face only when the performance earns it. Hands doing a
  specific task read as real; a smiling face reads as stock.
- **Sound design before music.** Real sounds from the scene (a pour, ceramic on wood, a
  kettle click) carry the mood. Music comes in late, if at all.
- **Dry over sentimental.** A line with a small, knowing edge beats a line that tells people
  how to feel.
- **The product is a tool, then a hero.** It's shown in use, doing its job, before it gets
  its clean product shot in the last seconds.
- **One real detail per scene** that only this place or person would have: a chipped mug, a
  sticky note on the fridge, a specific brand of kettle scale.

## What we reject

- Anyone gazing out a window holding a mug.
- Sun "flooding" a spotless kitchen. Real kitchens have a dish rack and a charging cable.
- A voiceover that sounds like a tagline being read aloud.
- Slow motion used to make something look expensive.
- Endings that fade to a logo on a colored background with no product in the frame.

## How to use these notes

Cite the specific note when a choice follows from it, and say so explicitly when you break
one on purpose and why.
